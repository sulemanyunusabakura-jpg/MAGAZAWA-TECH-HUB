Solar Laptop Charger

Microcontroller-based solar charging and monitoring system for laptops.

Project Overview

The Solar Laptop Charger is a renewable energy system designed to provide electrical power for charging a laptop using energy obtained from a solar panel.

The system combines solar energy generation, battery storage, voltage conversion and microcontroller-based monitoring.

Aim of the Project

The aim of this project is to design a microcontroller-based solar charging system capable of supplying a suitable DC output for laptop charging while monitoring important electrical parameters.

Objectives

  • Convert solar energy into usable electrical energy.
  • Store solar energy in a battery.
  • Produce a suitable DC voltage for the laptop charging system.
  • Monitor voltage and current.
  • Display system measurements.
  • Improve the usefulness of solar energy for portable electronics.

Main Components

  • Solar panel
  • Rechargeable battery
  • Battery management/protection system
  • Charge controller
  • DC-DC boost converter
  • Arduino Uno
  • Voltage sensor
  • Current sensor
  • LCD or OLED display
  • Connecting wires and protection components

How the System Works

The solar panel converts sunlight into electrical energy. The generated energy passes through the charging system and is used to charge the battery.

The stored battery energy is then supplied to a DC-DC converter. The converter adjusts the voltage to the level required by the laptop charging system.

The Arduino monitors selected electrical parameters and displays useful information to the user.

Solar Panel

The solar panel serves as the primary energy source. The size and electrical rating of the panel must be selected according to the expected charging power and environmental conditions.

A practical prototype can use a solar panel in the approximate 30–50 W range, depending on the intended design and laptop power requirements.

Battery Storage

A rechargeable battery stores energy generated by the solar panel so that the system can continue supplying power when sunlight is reduced.

The battery must be used with an appropriate charging controller and protection system suitable for its chemistry and configuration.

DC-DC Boost Converter

A boost converter can increase a lower DC battery voltage to a higher voltage required by the laptop charging system.

For example, when the battery-side voltage is lower than the required output voltage, the boost converter raises the voltage to the required level.

Voltage and Current Monitoring

The microcontroller can monitor the system using suitable voltage and current sensing circuits.

  • Solar panel voltage
  • Solar panel current
  • Battery voltage
  • Output voltage
  • Output current

Power Calculation

Electrical power can be calculated using:

P = V × I

Where:

  • P = Power in watts (W)
  • V = Voltage in volts (V)
  • I = Current in amperes (A)

For example, an output of 19 V at 2 A corresponds to:

P = 19 × 2 = 38 W

PWM-Based Charging Control

A PWM-based control approach can be used to regulate charging power in a suitable charging circuit.

The microcontroller can monitor system conditions and adjust the control signal according to the design requirements.

Display Information

The display can provide useful information such as:

  • Solar voltage
  • Solar current
  • Battery voltage
  • Output voltage
  • Charging status

Applications

  • Off-grid laptop charging
  • Renewable energy education
  • Engineering student projects
  • Portable power systems
  • Solar energy experiments
